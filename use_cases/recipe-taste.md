# What is the best version of Kung Pao Chicken for my taste?

## Context

I want a definitive recipe for Kung Pao Chicken (宫保鸡丁), the Sichuan
classic — not a Westernized version with bell peppers and ketchup-style
sauce, and not a restaurant approximation either. I want the version a
serious Sichuanese cook would make at home, adjusted to my palate.

## My taste

- I love málà (numbing-spicy) but my tolerance is moderate: Sichuan
  peppercorn should be noticeable, not dominant. Two chilis' worth of
  heat is my ceiling for a weeknight dish.
- I dislike sweetness in savory dishes — no sugar-forward sauce.
- Texture matters more to me than presentation: I want the classic
  "lychee-shaped" tender chicken and crunchy peanuts, never soggy.
- I cook on a standard Western electric stove with a 12-inch carbon
  steel wok, so be realistic about wok hei.

## Constraints

- Weeknight-friendly: 45 minutes total including prep, once ingredients
  are in the pantry.
- No hard-to-source specialty items unless truly load-bearing; suggest
  a credible substitute otherwise (e.g., for Chinkiang vinegar).
- Serves 2 with rice, scales to 4.
- I can find fresh Sichuan peppercorns and dried chilis at my local
  Asian grocer; chicken thighs are preferred over breast.

## The request

Give me one final recipe — ingredient list with gram measures, the wok
sequence with timings, and the three decisions where reasonable cooks
disagree (velvet vs. not, whole vs. ground peppercorn, dark vs. light
soy balance) resolved explicitly with reasoning. Be concrete about
technique rather than generic, and flag the single step where beginners
most often ruin this dish.
